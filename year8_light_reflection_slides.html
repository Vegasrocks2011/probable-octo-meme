Vocabulary

Key terms you need to know

Incident ray
The ray of light that travels towards the mirror surface.
From the Latin incidere — to fall upon
Reflected ray
The ray of light that bounces away from the mirror surface after striking it.
Always drawn with an arrow pointing away
The normal
An imaginary dashed line drawn at exactly 90° to the mirror surface at the point of incidence.
Always drawn as a dashed line — it isn't a real ray!
Angle of incidence
The angle between the incident ray and the normal. Labelled i.
⚠️ NOT the angle from the mirror surface
Angle of reflection
The angle between the reflected ray and the normal. Labelled r. This always equals the angle of incidence.
Both angles measured from the same line — the normal
The Law

The law of reflection

angle of incidence = angle of reflection
Both angles measured from the normal to the mirror surface
In symbols
i = r
where i and r are measured in degrees (°)
Common mistake ⚠️

Students often measure angles from the mirror surface instead of the normal. This gives 90° − the correct angle. Always use the dashed normal line!

If i = 30°
r = 30°
If i = 55°
r = 55°
If i = 0°
r = 0°
ray bounces straight back!

Practical

Ray-box investigation

Method
1

Place the plane mirror on the line marked on your paper. Draw the mirror line with a pencil.

2

Draw the normal at 90° to the mirror at the centre using a set square.

3

Set the ray box to your first angle of incidence (10°). Mark two dots on the incident ray.

4

Mark two dots on the reflected ray. Remove the mirror and draw both rays with a ruler.

5

Measure the angle of reflection from the normal using a protractor. Record in your table.

6

Repeat for each angle in your table. Take a second reading for each angle.

Equipment you need
  • 💡 Ray box with single slit
  • 🪞 Plane (flat) mirror
  • 📐 Protractor
  • 📏 30 cm ruler
  • ✏️ Pencil and paper
⚠️

Key reminders

Always measure angles from the normal, never from the mirror. Mark two dots on each ray so you can draw them accurately after removing the mirror.
